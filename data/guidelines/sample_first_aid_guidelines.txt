FIRST AID GUIDELINES (SAMPLE EXTRACT FOR DEVELOPMENT/TESTING)

CHOKING (ADULT)
If an adult is choking and cannot speak, cough or breathe, act quickly. First,
encourage them to cough. If coughing does not clear the obstruction, give up to five
sharp back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand. If back
blows do not work, give up to five abdominal thrusts. Call 999 if the blockage does
not clear. Continue alternating five back blows and five abdominal thrusts until help
arrives or the object is dislodged.

CARDIAC ARREST / NOT BREATHING (ADULT)
If a person is unresponsive and not breathing normally, they may be in cardiac
arrest. Call 999 immediately and ask for an ambulance. Start cardiopulmonary
resuscitation (CPR). Push hard and fast in the centre of the chest at a rate of 100
to 120 compressions per minute. If an automated external defibrillator (AED) is
available, switch it on and follow its spoken instructions. Do not stop CPR until
professional help takes over or the person shows signs of life.

SEVERE BLEEDING
For severe external bleeding, call 999. Apply firm, direct pressure to the wound with
a clean cloth or dressing. If an object is embedded in the wound, do not remove it;
instead apply pressure around the object. Keep pressure applied until emergency
services arrive. Lie the person down and reassure them.

BURNS
For a burn, cool the burn under cool running water for at least 20 minutes. Remove
any jewellery or clothing near the burn unless it is stuck to the skin. Do not apply
creams, butter, or ice. Cover the burn loosely with cling film or a clean, non-fluffy
dressing. Seek medical help for large or deep burns, or burns to the face, hands, or
genitals.

HEART ATTACK
If someone may be having a heart attack, call 999 immediately. Help them to sit down
and rest in a comfortable position. If they are not allergic and it is available,
help them take a 300 mg aspirin and advise them to chew it slowly. Stay with them and
monitor their breathing until the ambulance arrives.

ANAPHYLAXIS (SEVERE ALLERGIC REACTION)
Anaphylaxis is a medical emergency. Call 999 immediately. If the person has an
adrenaline auto-injector (such as an EpiPen), help them use it without delay. Help
them lie down with their legs raised, unless they are having trouble breathing, in
which case let them sit up. A second dose may be given after five minutes if there is
no improvement and a second injector is available.

SEIZURES
During a seizure, protect the person from injury by moving nearby objects away. Do
not restrain them and do not put anything in their mouth. Cushion their head. Time the
seizure. Call 999 if the seizure lasts longer than five minutes, if another seizure
follows, or if the person is injured or does not regain consciousness.
